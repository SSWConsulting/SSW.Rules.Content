A team reporting AI assistant quality to stakeholders sees accuracy fall to 71% and customers helped reach 94% on its dashboard. Neither rate has a denominator. Three topics have never been checked, while complaint-led checks are mixed into this week's headline. The page cannot support a whole-assistant verdict.

**Show how much was checked, out of what, beside every AI quality rate.** Name the sampled population and expose what the checks leave out.

<endIntro />

## Show the funnel before the rate

For 8–14 October, Northwind counts:

* **Eligible: 1,240** — Questions in chats with the assistant switched on
* **Attempted: 1,105** — AI tries to answer; 135 go straight to a person
* **Answered: 870** — AI supplies an answer; another 235 go to a person mid-chat
* **Random checks: 61** — Randomly drawn from 718 answers in five included topics, with no two checks from the same chat

The funnel reveals 370 handovers. In the bad example, **Customers helped 94%** means 818/870 answered questions have no repeat contact within 24 hours. That proxy does not establish that customers were helped, and it excludes all 370 handovers. Name the observed outcome and wait until its follow-up window closes before counting it.

For the checks, a support agent answers without seeing AI's answer. Matching answers measure agreement, not proven correctness; [adjudicate disagreements against evidence](/disagreement-inbox-for-ai-quality).

## Label random-review coverage beside agreement

Show **47/61 agreement (77%)** beside two random-review coverage figures. **61/718 (8.5%)** describes random reviews within the five-topic sampling frame. **61/870 (7%)** compares those same reviews with all answered questions. Both coverage figures exclude targeted reviews, and neither makes the excluded topics represented. If you also report total review coverage, deduplicate random and targeted cases first.

Keep the query and sampling-frame definition together in source control under a name such as `support_coverage_v1`. Compute the numerator, denominator, exclusions and dates together. Every quality tile needs its numerator and denominator, sampling frame and definition version, exclusions and period. Leave an unmeasured period blank; never let AI guess its percentage. Changes to the sampled population need a visible definition version.

When checks are too few for the decision, show their counts and uncertainty, collect more independent checks and withhold a whole-assistant verdict. [Plan the sample size for a useful comparison](/dashboard-changes-real-or-noise).

## Keep targeted checks separate

Northwind also checks 18 returns answers after complaints; nine agree. Pooling them into this week gives 56/79 (71%), while last week's 55/64 (86%) has no targeted checks. The apparent 15-percentage-point fall compares different selection methods.

Show **Targeted complaint checks: 9/18** separately. Use a consistent sampling frame to [compare periods](/dashboard-changes-real-or-noise). Repeated customers or review batches may require analysis that accounts for clustering even when chat IDs differ. [Statistics Canada's sampling guidance](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/edu/power-pouvoir/ch13/prob/5214899-eng.htm) explains population lists, random selection and clustered designs.

## List topics outside the sample

Northwind's never-checked topics have 71 gift-card answers, 48 wholesale answers and 33 allergen answers: **152/870 (17.5%)**. List them visibly and prioritise review by risk as well as volume; an allergen answer can matter even at lower volume.
