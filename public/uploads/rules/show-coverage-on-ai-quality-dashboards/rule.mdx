A team reporting AI assistant quality to stakeholders sees accuracy fall to 71% and customers helped reach 94% on its dashboard. Neither rate has a denominator. Three topics have never been checked, while complaint-led checks are mixed into this week's headline. The page cannot support a whole-assistant verdict.

**Show how much was checked, out of what, beside every AI quality rate.** Name the sampled population and expose what the checks leave out.

<endIntro />

## Show the funnel before the rate

For 8–14 October, Northwind counts:

* **Eligible: 1,240** — Questions in chats with the assistant switched on
* **Attempted: 1,105** — The assistant tries to answer; 135 go straight to a person
* **Answered: 870** — The assistant supplies an answer; another 235 go to a person mid-chat

The funnel reveals 370 handovers. Review sampling is a separate step, taken from the answered questions.

## Label random-review coverage beside agreement

Northwind randomly reviews 61 of the 718 answers in five included topics, with no two checks from the same chat. A support agent answers without seeing the assistant's answer. Matching answers measure agreement, not proven correctness; [adjudicate disagreements against evidence](/disagreement-inbox-for-ai-quality).

Show **47/61 agreement (77%)** beside two random-review coverage figures. **61/718 (8.5%)** describes random reviews within the five-topic sampling frame. **61/870 (7%)** compares those same reviews with all answered questions. Both coverage figures exclude targeted reviews, and neither covers the three excluded topics. If you also report total review coverage, deduplicate random and targeted cases first.

A one-answer-per-chat design can give answers in longer chats a lower chance of selection. Record the design and any weighting; the unweighted 47/61 describes the reviewed answers.

When checks are too few for the decision, show their counts and uncertainty, collect more independent checks and withhold a whole-assistant verdict. [Plan the sample size for a useful comparison](/dashboard-changes-real-or-noise).

## Save what every quality tile means

Keep the query and sampling-frame definition together in source control under a name such as `support_coverage_v1`. Every quality tile needs its numerator and denominator, sampling frame, exclusions, period and visible definition version. Compute these together and change the version when the sampled population changes.

If a period has no completed reviews, label agreement **Not measured**. A guessed percentage would present an unchecked period as evidence. [Show missing and stale data explicitly](/show-missing-and-stale-data-on-dashboards) rather than asking a language model to fill the gap.

## Keep targeted checks separate

Northwind also checks 18 answers about returns, chosen after complaints; nine agree. Pooling them into this week gives 56/79 (71%), while last week's 55/64 (86%) has no targeted checks. The apparent 15-percentage-point fall compares different selection methods.

Show **Targeted complaint checks: 9/18** separately. Use a consistent sampling frame to [compare periods](/dashboard-changes-real-or-noise). Repeated customers or review batches may require analysis that accounts for clustering even when chat IDs differ. [Statistics Canada's sampling guidance](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/edu/power-pouvoir/ch13/prob/5214899-eng.htm) explains population lists, random selection and clustered designs.

## Name proxy outcomes honestly

In the bad example, **Customers helped 94%** means 818/870 answered questions have no repeat contact within 24 hours. That proxy does not establish that customers were helped, and it excludes all 370 handovers. Name the observed outcome and wait until its follow-up window closes before counting it.

## List topics outside the sample

Northwind's never-checked topics have 71 gift-card answers, 48 wholesale answers and 33 allergen answers: **152/870 (17.5%)**. List them visibly and prioritise review by risk as well as volume; an allergen answer can matter even at lower volume.
