Teams often pick a rendering strategy by framework default and move on. This rendering strategy often decides how fast your page loads, whether search engines can read them, how stale your content gets, and ultimately how much you pay your hosting provider. 

Get it wrong and you can end up server rendering a marketing page that changes twice a year, or shipping an your home page as an empty shell to Google when they go to rank it.

<endIntro />

## Why rendering strategy matters

In short, rendering strategy impacts **when,** **where,** and **how** your website is generated, affecting 6 key areas: 

1. Performance - Time-to-First-Byte (TTFB), First Contentful Paint (FCP), Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) and initial loading speed 
2. SEO - How easily search engines can discover and index your content 
3. Hosting costs - The rate at which a server needs to fulfill requests  
4. Scalability - How much server work is required as traffic increases
5. Content freshness - Whether users see real-time, cached or build time content
6. Personalization - Whether pages can be customized per user
