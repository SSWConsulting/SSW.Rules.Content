A suspicious login fires an alert at 2am. By lunch there are four email threads, two Teams chats and a forwarded screenshot. Someone asks "which IPs did we block?" and gets three different answers. The evidence exists, but it's scattered across inboxes, and anyone who wasn't CC'd never sees it.

Keep one incident page as the source of truth. Every log extract, screenshot, finding and decision goes there. Email becomes a snapshot that says what changed and links to the page.

<endIntro />

## Why shouldn't email be your source of truth?

Email is a copy, not a record. Once it's sent you can't correct it, and every reply forks the story.

* **Stale the moment it's sent** - The 10am update still says "no data accessed" after the 2pm finding proves otherwise
* **Evidence lives in attachments** - The only copy of the log export sits in the inbox of someone who's now on leave
* **Late joiners miss evidence** - The security lead brought in on day 2 only sees the threads they were forwarded
* **No clean timeline** - Your post-incident review starts with an afternoon of stitching replies together

## What goes on the incident page?

Create the page the moment you declare the incident. Use Confluence, SharePoint or whatever your team already lives in. Keep it to four parts:

* **Status** - Current severity, incident owner and the time of the next update, at the top
* **Timeline** - A timestamped entry for every finding, action and decision
* **Evidence** - Log extracts, screenshots and file hashes, each noting who collected it and when
* **Actions** - What's contained, what's outstanding and who owns each item

## What should the email say?

What changed since the last update, when the next one is due, and the link. Nothing else.
