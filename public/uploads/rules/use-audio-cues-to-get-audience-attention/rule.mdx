A lot of your audience will not always be looking at your video. A curated sound can make users focus on the changes in a demo.

<endIntro />

### Sound 1 - “Paste” sound to highlight content additions

When you paste text, code snippets, or configurations during a fast-paced demo, viewers can easily miss where the new content came from. A quick, subtle paste sound draws immediate attention to the change on the screen.

<youtubeEmbed description="✅ Video: Good example - Audio cue highlights the exact moment content is pasted" url="https://youtu.be/lgrI3tcyFUg" />

Microsoft has plenty of similar built-in sounds for saving, printing and more. It's always better to use audio cues your audience already recognizes.

<imageEmbed alt="Image" size="medium" showBorder={false} figurePrefix="none" figure="If yours are silent, turn them on in Word/Excel → Preferences → General → Play sounds for events such as opening, saving, or printing." src="/uploads/rules/use-audio-cues-to-get-audience-attention/Rule-audio-cue.png" />

### Sound 2 - “Typing” sound to make on-screen typing feel alive

When you show someone typing a command, a prompt, or a line of code, silent keystrokes make the screen feel flat and pre-recorded. A light typing sound tells viewers that something is being entered right now, and keeps their eyes on the cursor instead of drifting.

<youtubeEmbed url="https://youtu.be/mDGmCN_ftcA?si=1FFh8XyKBZrK28fX&t=93" description="✅ Video: Good example - Typing sound follows the keystrokes as the prompt is entered" />

### Sound 3 - Sound Effects to highlight important milestone events

Audio cues are not only useful when text appears — they are also powerful when highlighting **important milestone events** during a demo or tutorial.

Viewers are often multitasking or looking away from the screen. A short sound effect acts as an **attention trigger**, letting them know something meaningful has just happened. This is especially valuable when the event involves **executables or results**, rather than simple navigation.

<boxEmbed
  body={<>
    Use audio cues primarily for **milestones or meaningful outcomes**, not for simple navigation (such as clicking menus). Overusing sounds can make them distracting instead of helpful.
  </>}
  figurePrefix="none"
  figure=""
  style="info"
/>

Examples of milestone events include clicks to:

* Deploy to release an application
* Submit to send data or complete a form
* Generate to create a report, document, or artifact
* Build to compile a project
* Create when a new resource (project, repository, environment) is being created
* Connect or Authenticate to establish a service connection
* Start recording or Start demo in a tutorial
* Install to start installing software

These moments represent **milestones in the story of the demo**. Adding a subtle audio cue reinforces that something important occurred.
