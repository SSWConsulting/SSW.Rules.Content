When a video moves to a story from the past or a bad example, viewers can miss the change if every shot looks the same. They may even mistake what they are seeing for the present situation or the recommended approach.

<endIntro />

<youtubeEmbed url="https://youtu.be/CSDWclD5W_k?t=22" description="✅ Video: Good example - From 0:22, each bad response is in black and white, then color returns for the better response" />

Black and white gives viewers a visual cue that this part is different. Returning to color then shows that the video has moved back to the present or to a better approach.

## When to use black and white

Use black and white for these 2 situations.

### In the past

Use black and white for a historical story or the way things used to be.

For example, in "Treat Your Code Like Uranium", Adam tells a story about 2 factories from the Second World War. The factory footage is in black and white, which separates the story from the present.

<youtubeEmbed url="https://youtu.be/MgeB2mKM8jU?t=12" description="✅ Video: Good example - At 0:12, black and white factory footage shows that the story is from the past" />

### Bad examples

Use black and white for a problem, a bad experience, or something viewers should avoid.

For example, in "Creativity under Pressure", Anton asks viewers to imagine the worst picture, with tight deadlines and constant interruptions. The stock footage of this high-pressure workplace is in black and white, so it looks different from the color footage around it.

<youtubeEmbed url="https://youtu.be/qRUW3zes4Gk?t=130" description="✅ Video: Good example - At 2:10, black and white footage shows the high-pressure workplace as the negative situation" />
