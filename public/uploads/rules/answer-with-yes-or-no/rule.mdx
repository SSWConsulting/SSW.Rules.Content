When someone asks a yes or no question, a long answer makes them dig for what they want to know, and it can sound like you are avoiding the question. Start with 'Yes' or 'No', then follow with the details.

<endIntro />

<youtubeEmbed url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CSDWclD5W_k" description="Video: Just Say Yes or No! 3 Tips for Better Communication | Adam Cogan (2 min)" />

## Start with 'Yes' or 'No'

If the answer is 'No', say so first. Then explain where things are up to and what you will do next.

<boxEmbed
  style="greybox"
  body={<>
    **Adam:** Have you deployed the software?
  </>}
  figurePrefix="none"
  figure=""
/>

<boxEmbed
  style="greybox"
  body={<>
    **George:** Well, I made the changes and then I uploaded it to this server and then I ran this.
  </>}
  figurePrefix="bad"
  figure="A lot of words, but no clear answer"
/>

<boxEmbed
  style="greybox"
  body={<>
    **George:** No.
  </>}
  figurePrefix="ok"
  figure="A clear answer, but it doesn't explain the status or what happens next"
/>

<boxEmbed
  style="greybox"
  body={<>
    **George:** No, I haven't. But I have made the changes and given them to Bob, and I'm waiting for the PR. I should have followed him up, so I'll do that right now.
  </>}
  figurePrefix="good"
  figure="The answer comes first, followed by the status and the next step"
/>

## Say 'Understood' when you are corrected

Being corrected is never nice, but a list of excuses sounds like you are arguing. Say 'Understood', then give the reason in a few words.

<boxEmbed
  style="greybox"
  body={<>
    **Adam:** You should have deployed the software last night.
  </>}
  figurePrefix="none"
  figure=""
/>

<boxEmbed
  style="greybox"
  body={<>
    **George:** Well, I've been really busy and I'm super tired and Mary's been stressing me out.
  </>}
  figurePrefix="bad"
  figure="Excuses make the conversation longer and don't solve anything"
/>

<boxEmbed
  style="greybox"
  body={<>
    **George:** Understood. I think I didn't do it because I got distracted with Mary's problem.
  </>}
  figurePrefix="good"
  figure="Accepts the correction and gives a short reason"
/>

## Don't get defensive

Getting moody, shutting down, being defensive or using sarcasm only makes things worse. If you forgot, say so and be genuine about it. That defuses the situation and everyone can move on.

<boxEmbed
  style="greybox"
  body={<>
    **George:** I forgot.
  </>}
  figurePrefix="good"
  figure="A simple, genuine answer"
/>

Whether or not it was your fault, [acknowledge it, apologize and explain what you will do next](/how-to-take-feedback-or-criticism).
