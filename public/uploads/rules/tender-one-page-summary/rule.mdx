Tender documents often run to hundreds of pages across many files. If the Bid Manager has not got their head around them by the Go/No-Go meeting, the team makes a big decision on a vague idea, and surprises such as a fixed price turn up in the last week.

<endIntro />

On day 1, get an AI skill to read every tender document and draft a one-page summary. The Bid Manager then checks it against the source documents and corrects it.

## What goes on the page

Focus on what changes the commercial decision:

* **The client and their problem** - 1 or 2 sentences
* **Key dates** - closing date, clarification question deadline, contract start
* **Pricing model** - fixed price, time and materials, or a mix. ⚠️ Call out a fixed price at the top
* **Budget** - if the tender states one
* **Mandatory requirements** - the "must haves" that knock you out if you miss them
* **Evaluation criteria** - and their weightings
* **Contract risks** - for example, liability, penalties, or IP ownership
* **Understanding** - a % for how much of the tender you understand, and what the unknowns are

Do not worry too much about the technical details. The Pre-sales Solution Architect owns the technical solution. The Bid Manager needs to understand the commercial shape of the bid.

## State how much you understand

End the summary with an honest % and the gaps. This tells the Bid Owner how much to trust the summary, and it lists what to find out before Gate 1.
