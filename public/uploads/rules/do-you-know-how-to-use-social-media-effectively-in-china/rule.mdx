The biggest mistake most western companies make when embarking on a business journey in China, is assuming that their current Marketing strategies will work over there.

China is different. There are different rules, platforms and of course, they have a government controlled firewall that blocks many western platforms your are familiar with, like Facebook, X, Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube. Uh oh. That's right - none of the platforms you currently use everyday now work in China, you need a new plan.

<endIntro />

China has a huge amount of social media users, there are 1.28 billion active users (2025) - which is huge! It is a huge opportunity for market growth, particularly for app owners who would like to expand their product to millions of users. 

<imageEmbed alt="Image" size="large" showBorder={false} figurePrefix="none" figure="There are 1.28 billion social media users in China, 90.3% of the overall population" src="/uploads/rules/do-you-know-how-to-use-social-media-effectively-in-china/Rule-1.png" />

There is only one big problem, China has hundreds of platforms, and none of them is a straight swap for a western one. Baidu is not just "Chinese Google". RedNote is not "Chinese Instagram". If you treat them as copies, you will make the wrong content for all of them.

Do you know the best platforms and how to use them in China? Here is a list of our top 6:

## 1. Baidu (Google Search of China)

90% of online experiences in the Western world begin with a Google Search. In China it is Baidu that dominates, with about 58% of online search starting there, and 72% on phones. That's billions of searches per day!

If you already run Google Ads, Baidu Ads works the same way — you bid on keywords and pay per click. But there are 2 differences that catch people out:

* You pay first. Google bills you later. Baidu wants the money in the account before your ads go live
* Baidu checks who you are. Google needs an email and a credit card. Baidu needs your business licence, and locks one account to one company and one website

## 2. RedNote (Instagram + Pinterest of China)

RedNote, aka Xiaohongshu, is a leading Chinese social media and e-commerce platform combining lifestyle sharing and online shopping. Founded in 2013, it now has over 400 million monthly active users, mainly young and urban. The app features photo and video posts on fashion, beauty, travel, and daily life, with a sleek, Instagram-like design. Users can discover trends, share authentic reviews, and buy products directly through the platform.

Here is the part that matters for marketing: people come to RedNote to look things up. Before a Chinese consumer buys anything, a face cream, a laptop, a holiday, a dentist. They look it up here first and read what real users said. If your brand has nothing on RedNote, you lose the argument before it starts.

Once you have an account, you can:

* Post — notes and short videos that show up in search
* Advertise — paid placements in the feed and search results
* Sell — run a store inside the app, no website needed
* Go live — livestream selling, which is huge in China
* Work with KOLs — most discovery happens through influencers and everyday users, not brand accounts

## 3. Douyin (Tiktok of China)

Douyin is the app Tiktok was built from, same company, same look, but a separate platform that only runs in mainland China. It reaches almost 1 billion people, which is most of the country.

2 things TikTok does not have:

* A real shop inside the app. Videos, livestreams and product pages all feed one Douyin Store. The whole purchase happens without ever leaving Douyin
* Local services. People book restaurants, hotels and tickets on Douyin the way they would on Meituan. If you have a physical place in China, this is how people find it

## 4. Bilibili (YouTube + Reddit of China)

We all know the best marketers use video to sell their products and talk to their audience directly. In the West that means YouTube. YouTube is blocked in China, and the closest thing is Bilibili.

What started as China's largest anime streaming site has grown into a video community for young Chinese audiences. It has 376 million monthly active users, and 115 million of them watch Bilibili almost 2 hours everyday. 

Comments fly across the screen while the video plays, viewers argue with each other in real time, and a good creator is treated as a member of the community rather than a channel. That part is much closer to Reddit.

According to Bilibili's annual report to investors, their top 5 most watched video categories are:

1. Entertainment
2. Lifestyle
3. Gaming
4. Anime
5. Technology

## 5. Weibo (Twitter of China)

Weibo is a social media platform where people go not only to post, share and interact with each other, but also to follow trends and see the news. With 561 million monthly active users, this is the place for content to go viral and for Chinese influencers, known as KOLs, to build their online communities. Like Facebook Live, it has its own livestream platform which is often used for product promotion.

Douyin and RedNote have taken a lot of Weibo's attention in recent years, but 254 million people still open it every day, and it is still where news breaks and trends start. Think of it as your news and PR channel, the place you announce things and see what people are saying about the world.

## 6. WeChat (The Chinese Super App)

WeChat started as an instant messaging app similar to WhatsApp but has morphed into a ‘super app’ that now allows you to do just about anything! Users can perform a wide variety of activities such as making transactions, ordering food, booking movie tickets, calling a taxi, even playing games. It now has more than 1.4 billion active users.

The other 5 platforms bring in strangers. WeChat is where they become clients. This is what China calls your private domain — the audience you own, rather than the audience a platform rents to you. A Service Account sends your updates, a Mini Program takes bookings and payments without anyone installing anything, and your sales team talks to clients in the same app the clients already live in. In China, business relationships live in WeChat, not in email.
