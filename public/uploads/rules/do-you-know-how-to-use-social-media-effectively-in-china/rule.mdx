The biggest mistake most western companies make when embarking on a business journey in China, is assuming that their current Marketing strategies will work over there.

China is different. There are different rules, platforms and of course, they have a government controlled firewall that blocks many western platforms your are familiar with, like Facebook, X, Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube. \*Uh oh\*. That's right - none of the platforms you currently use everyday now work in China, you need a new plan.

<endIntro />

China has a huge amount of social media users, there are 1.28 billion active users (2025) - which is huge!  It is a huge opportunity for market growth, particularly for app owners who would like to expand their product to millions of users.

<imageEmbed alt="Image" size="large" showBorder={false} figurePrefix="none" figure="According to DataReportal, there are 1.28 billion social media users in China, 90.3% of the overall population (Digital 2026: China, data to October 2025)" src="/uploads/rules/do-you-know-how-to-use-social-media-effectively-in-china/Rule-1.png" />

There is one big problem, China has hundreds of platforms, and none of them is a straight swap for a western one. Baidu is not just "Chinese Google". RedNote is not "Chinese Instagram". If you treat them as copies, you will make the wrong content for all of them.

Do you know the best platforms and how to use them in China? Here is a list of our top 6:

## 1. 

## WeChat (The Chinese Super App)

WeChat started as an instant messaging app similar to WhatsApp but has morphed into a ‘super app’ that now allows you to do just about anything! Users can perform a wide variety of activities such as making transactions, ordering food, booking movie tickets, calling a taxi, even playing games. It now has more than 1 billion active users.

<imageEmbed alt="Image" size="large" showBorder={false} figurePrefix="none" figure="Taken from Statistica.com - the Graph shows how much WeChat is growing every year. Why you should consider using it:

* 68M videos are uploaded every day
* WeChat users send averagely 45 billion messages daily
* On average, users spend more than 66 minutes on WeChat daily.
* 60% of WeChat users open the app more than 10 times a day.
* By the end of 2019, the number of WeChat’s Official Accounts surpassed 20M.
* Flexible media – you can upload pictures, text, short videos, articles etc.

There are 4 types of Official  WeChat Accounts; “Subscription Accounts”, “Service Accounts", “Enterprise Accounts" and the “Mini Program”. Each of them has unique functions and uses, which are outlined in more detail on [creating a WeChat official account](/how-to-create-wechat-official-account).

## 2. Weibo (The Twitter of China)

Weibo is an social media platform where people go not only post, share and interact with each other, but also follow trends and see the news. This is the place for content to go viral and for Chinese Influences, known as KOLs, to build their online communities. Like Facebook Live, It has its own live stream platform which is often used for product promotion. Conveniently it also has E-commerce integration which mean that people can link their accounts to Alipay and purchase products with one click.

Why you should consider using it:

* Number of Weibo active users: 584M (2023)
* Number of daily users: 253M (2023)
* 50% increase in livestream sessions
* Q\&A postings increased by 400% in 2018 compared to 2017
* 82 per cent of Weibo users shop online

## 3. Youku (The YouTube of China)

Often referred to as the YouTube of China, Youku is a video hosting service that is great for long-form videos. While there are popular short-form video platforms, like Tik Tok, Youku has remained the popular source of videos for people who want to gain greater knowledge in a subject, or to watch the news.

Why you should consider using it:

* 580M active users, who on average visit it daily 39 times.
* Over 1 billion video views per day
* On average people stay for around 4 minutes per video
* It will help you reach a wider audience and build a reputation as a source of knowledge
* It’s great for brand awareness via video content
* It can be used to drive traffic to your website and increase sales

## 1. Baidu (The Google Search of China)

90% of online experiences in the Western world begin with a Google Search. In China it is Baidu that dominates, with about 58% of online search starting there, and 72% on phones. That's billions of searches per day!

*If you want to use Baidu, here is what you can do *first*:*

* Search your company name in Chinese on Baidu. What comes up is your China brand today.
* Build a Chinese website. Write it in Chinese — do not translate your English one. 90% of Baidu traffic is mobile, so design for the phone.
* Host it in mainland China and get an ICP filing. Overseas hosting is slow, and Baidu will not approve your ads without it.
* Pick your keywords in Chinese. Your English keyword list will not translate. Use Baidu Index (index.baidu.com) to see what people actually search.

Once that is done you can run a campaign, same idea as Google Ads — with two differences. 

* You pay up front, not after. And Baidu only opens ad accounts for mainland Chinese companies, so without one you go through a licensed Baidu agent. 
* To start, budget CNY 5,000 to open the account, or CNY 6,000 through an agent. Minimum spend is CNY 100 a day, no monthly commitment.

## 5. Bilibili (Up & coming alternate to Youku)

We all know that the best marketers are using video to sell their products and directly engage their audience online. Brands should not overlook the usefulness of branded online video for their audience. Similar to how YouTube experiences huge popularity in the West, China’s video platforms such as Youku and iQiyi are successful with the local Chinese audience. But one live streaming platform that is often overlooked is the video-sharing platform Bilibili.

What started as a China's largest Anime streaming platform, it has grown to be one of the most popular video sharing platforms for China’s Millennials and Generation Z with over 100 million monthly active users, where users can view, submit, and add commentary to videos.

Bilibili also recently launched a premium membership enabling access to special shows, e-commerce deals, and HQ video streaming. If you want to increase your long-term success in China, it is important to penetrate Gen-Z consumers who have an expanding purchasing power. Bilibili, uses mini programs (multi-page application containing a complete application feature), livestreaming, and e-commerce to benefit brands looking to market to younger consumers.

## 6. RedNote (Instagram + Pinterest of China)

RedNote, aka Xiaohongshu or RedBook, is a leading Chinese social media and e-commerce platform combining lifestyle sharing and online shopping. Founded in 2013, it has over 300 million monthly active users, mainly young and urban. The app features photo and video posts on fashion, beauty, travel, and daily life, with a sleek, Instagram-like design. Users can discover trends, share authentic reviews, and buy products directly through the platform. RedNote has become a powerful influencer marketing space and a major lifestyle guide, shaping consumer choices and popular culture across China and beyond.

Why you should use it:

* Strong Trendsetting Impact
* Over 300 million active users
* Highly Engaged User Base
* Powerful Influencer Ecosystem
* Integrated E-commerce Features
