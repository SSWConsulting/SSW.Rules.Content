An AI draft may use a tone you would never use with your readers. Examples of your own writing can give it a more useful starting point than a request to "sound human."

Use those examples to describe your preferences, then review the result. A style match is approximate. It does not guarantee authenticity or accuracy.

<endIntro />

## How do examples guide a draft?

Giving examples in a prompt supplies context for the response. It is different from fine-tuning a model or changing its underlying weights. [Few-shot learning research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165) describes using textual demonstrations without gradient updates.

Start with writing that fits the task. An informal message to a colleague is a poor model for a detailed report if the audiences need different things. Use samples you are authorized to share, and remove confidential information unless its use is approved.

## What should you ask the AI to notice?

Ask for observations about tone, vocabulary, sentence structure, punctuation, and how you explain an argument. Request examples from the samples so you can check the analysis. A model may mistake one document's quirks for your general preferences.

Keep the notes you agree with. Preserve deliberate habits, including longer explanations, three-part lists, or dashes when they serve the reader. You do not need to remove a useful device because a model also uses it.

## How do you use the style notes?

Give the AI the approved notes together with the new task's audience, purpose, facts, and sources. Choose the length the task needs instead of asking it to fill an arbitrary word count.

<boxEmbed
  style="greybox"
  body={<>
    Draft from the supplied notes and sources for this audience. Use the approved style notes as guidance. Preserve the facts, uncertainty, terminology, and position I have supplied. Do not invent quotations, achievements, or personal experience. Flag missing information. Include enough detail to answer the reader's questions without repeating points.
  </>}
  figurePrefix="good"
  figure="Voice guidance supplements the brief; it does not replace evidence"
/>

Compare the draft with your intentions and sources. A familiar voice can still contain unsupported claims or commitments you did not make. Use the [meaning-preserving editing workflow](/humanise-ai-generated-content/) before accepting the result.

## How can you reuse the notes?

Save the approved notes as reusable instructions in your chosen tool. Revisit them when the audience or writing task changes. A stored style guide remains fallible, and style similarity does not establish who wrote a passage or what assistance must be disclosed.
