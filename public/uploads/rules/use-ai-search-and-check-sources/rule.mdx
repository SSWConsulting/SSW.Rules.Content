AI search tools such as Perplexity, ChatGPT search, Google AI Mode and Copilot answer a question in a paragraph with citations, instead of giving you a list of links to read. That saves a lot of time on research, but the answer is only as good as its sources, and AI search tools still get those wrong surprisingly often.

<endIntro />

## Pick the right search for the job

No AI search tool is clearly better than the others, and most browsers now have one built in, so the tool matters less than knowing when to use it.

### Use AI search 

When you need an answer that is spread across several pages:

* Research questions, e.g. "What changed in .NET 10 for minimal APIs?"
* Comparing options, e.g. "Azure Container Apps vs App Service for a small API"
* Getting up to speed on an unfamiliar topic before you read the primary sources

### Use classic search 

When you already know what you are looking for:

* Navigating to a known site or page
* Finding an exact phrase, error message or file name
* Local results, shopping and anything where you want to compare the raw results yourself

## Always check the sources

AI search makes citation mistakes with full confidence. In a [2025 Tow Center study of eight AI search tools](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php), more than 60% of answers about where a news excerpt came from were wrong, and even the best tool was wrong 37% of the time. Some tools linked to pages that didn't exist.

Before you rely on an AI search answer:

1. **Open the cited pages** - Check that each source exists and actually says what the answer claims
2. **Prefer primary sources** - Official docs, the original study or the vendor announcement beat a blog post that summarizes them
3. **Check the date** - AI answers often mix current and outdated information, especially for fast-moving products
4. **Cite the source, not the AI** - When you share the information, link the page you verified
