A full stop or slash at the end of a URL can send people to the wrong place. When someone copies the URL, or an app turns it into a link, the full stop can come with it, and the reader gets a "page not found" error. A trailing slash can make the same page available at two different addresses.

<endIntro />

## Use a descriptive link instead

The simplest fix is not to show the raw URL at all. Use a [descriptive link](/descriptive-links), so there's no URL at the end of the sentence for a full stop to break.

## Don't put a full stop after a URL

If you must show the raw URL, leave the full stop off, even at the end of a sentence. Or rewrite the sentence so the URL isn't the last thing in it.

<boxEmbed
  style="greybox"
  body={<>
    For more information, visit [https://www.ssw.com.au/employment](https://www.ssw.com.au/employment).
  </>}
  figurePrefix="bad"
  figure="The full stop can end up in the link when it's copied or clicked"
/>

<boxEmbed
  style="greybox"
  body={<>
    For more information, visit [https://www.ssw.com.au/employment](https://www.ssw.com.au/employment)
  </>}
  figurePrefix="good"
  figure="No full stop after the URL"
/>

## Don't add a trailing slash

`https://www.ssw.com.au/employment` and `https://www.ssw.com.au/employment/` are two different URLs. [Google treats them as separate pages](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2010/04/to-slash-or-not-to-slash), so if both load the same content, search engines can see a duplicate page and split its ranking between the two.

Leave the slash off when you write or share a link. On your own site, pick one form and redirect the other to it. For example, `https://www.ssw.com.au/employment/` redirects to the version without the slash. A slash straight after the domain name, as in `https://www.ssw.com.au/`, is fine.

  style="greybox"
  body={<>
    `https://www.ssw.com.au/employment/`

  figurePrefix="bad"
  figure="The trailing slash makes a second URL for the same page"
/>

    `https://www.ssw.com.au/employment`

  figurePrefix="good"
