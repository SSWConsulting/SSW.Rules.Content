R&D reporting is slow because the evidence is scattered across commits, pull requests, issues and people's memories. It is usually pieced together months after the work, when details have been forgotten.

AI agents are good at the gathering, cross-referencing and first-draft parts of this job. They are not good at deciding what is true, so a human must stay accountable for the claim.

<endIntro />

## 1. Gather evidence from your repos

Point an agent at your repositories and have it collect, for each core activity:

* Merged pull requests
* Key commits
* Related issues and PBIs
* Abandoned pull requests and approaches that were not adopted. See [Do you document your failures for R&D?](/do-you-record-your-failures)

Ask it to keep every PR, commit and issue link, so each statement in the write-up can be checked.

## 2. Mine your AI chat history

Reasoning often never reaches a commit message or PR. Point the agent at your past AI chats and coding-agent sessions to recover:

* The architecture, and how it evolved
* The decisions made, and the options you rejected and why
* What you expected to happen (useful for the hypothesis)

Many coding agents keep session transcripts on disk, so they can be searched.

## 3. Draft the write-up

Have the agent draft each core activity in the structure the claim needs:

* Description of the activity
* Technical goals
* Hypothesis
* Experiments
* Results
* New knowledge generated

Start from the core activities your team defined. See [Do you define your core activities for R&D?](/do-you-define-your-core-activities) and [Do you know the three parts of R&D work?](/know-the-three-parts-of-rd).

## 4. Review it adversarially

Use a separate agent, with fresh context, to check the draft against the R&D criteria and verify that every citation supports the sentence it is attached to. An agent that wrote the draft will not reliably find its own mistakes.

## 5. Keep a human accountable

AI must not make the claim stronger than the record supports.

* **Don't invent measurements.** If a number only exists in telemetry (for example, App Insights or PostHog), list it as an outstanding evidence request and retrieve it
* **Don't claim a hypothesis was documented in advance when it wasn't.** State the hypothesis plainly. On the claim form, leave a supporting-records box unticked if the document does not exist. An unticked box is safer than a false tick
* **Check the citations.** Open the links and confirm they say what the draft says they do
* **A named person owns the final claim**

## 6. Disclose AI-assisted development

R&D work done with AI tools should say so. Use a consistent marker, such as a commit trailer or a footer in the PR description, so the number of AI-assisted pull requests can be counted accurately.

Check your counts against the full source. If your tooling truncates long PR bodies, it can cut off the footer and understate the figure.
