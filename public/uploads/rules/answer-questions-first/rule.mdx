We're all in lots of group chats, and when you have a question, you usually just ask it. But if someone asked a question last week and nobody answered, your new message buries it even deeper. They are still waiting, and it looks like nobody is reading the chat.

<endIntro />

<youtubeEmbed
  url="https://www.youtube.com/shorts/2oKYOkhsGcw"

/>

A chat only works when questions get answers. Taking a few seconds to sort out what's already there unblocks your teammates sooner, shows you are reading what others write, and stops important questions from quietly getting lost.

## Check for anything unanswered

Before you post a new question in a Teams chat, check for any questions that never got a reply.

<imageEmbed
  src="/uploads/rules/answer-questions-first/bad-new-question-buries-old-one.png"
  alt="A Teams group chat where a new question is posted below a question from last week that never got a reply"
  size="large"
  showBorder={true}
  figurePrefix="bad"
  figure="Bad example - Asking without checking first buries last week's unanswered question"
/>

## Sort that out first

If you find an unanswered question, sort it out before you ask yours, even when it wasn't asked of you. Answer it if you can. If you can't, reply to the original message and @mention someone who can.

Once that's done, go ahead and ask your question. The other person is sorted, and you still get your answer.

  src="/uploads/rules/answer-questions-first/good-answer-old-question-first.png"

/>

## Ask about discussions that never wrapped up

Sometimes a discussion starts, a few people reply, and then it just stops. There is no decision and no clear next step. If an earlier discussion never wrapped up, ask about that first too.

<imageEmbed
  src="/uploads/rules/answer-questions-first/good-ask-status-of-unfinished-discussion.png"
  alt="A Teams group chat where a discussion from last week ended without an outcome and someone asks what was decided"
  size="large"

/>
