Ask AI for a "next week" forecast and it adds a smooth line to the dashboard in minutes, often from a popular forecasting library. Nobody knows how wrong that line usually is, yet the warehouse and support leads roster staff around it. Meanwhile the current week, only half logged, looks like a collapse.

That's why you should **backtest every forecast and show its typical miss before it goes on a dashboard**.

## Backtest against simple baselines

A backtest replays history: forecast each past week using only the data that existed then, and record the miss. Test forecasts as far ahead as the dashboard predicts. Northwind Traders' "next week" is 2 weeks past its latest full week, so its backtest forecasts each of the last 52 weeks from 2 weeks earlier.

Put simple baselines beside whatever model AI proposes: the latest full week, the average of the last 4 weeks, and the same week last year plus growth. Pick the method with the smallest typical miss for each series. The library model AI suggested first wins support chats at ±11%, but for agent hours the plain "same week last year, plus growth" beats it (±9% against ±19%).

## Show the typical miss beside the forecast

The typical miss is the error a method stays within in 4 of 5 past weeks. Show it as "Usually within ±X%" with a plain label: **Good** at 10% or less, **Rough** above that. Northwind's support chats tile reads 3,010 (2,680–3,340), "Usually within ±11% · Rough", so the support lead rosters with room to spare.

## Feed in what you already know

Some of next week is already decided: promotions are scheduled, pre-orders taken and leave booked. Adding them cuts the typical miss for orders to ±9% (against ±14% for the library model) and for agent hours to ±3% (against ±9%). The untested forecast said 7,659 orders. The backtested one, which knows about 3 promotions, says 10,170.

## Prove the backtest can't peek

A backtest that can see the future means nothing. Prove the check by leaking future data on purpose and confirming it gets caught. Northwind planted 3 leaks and caught all 3. One of them, a 4-week average that included the week being forecast, scored ±9% on support chats instead of its honest ±14%.

## Check what each source leaves out

* **Incomplete weeks** - The current week shows as "logged so far" and stays out of the forecast until it closes
* **Missing records** - The leave system holds only 58% of leave taken (checked against payroll), so the forecast adds typical unbooked leave
* **Noise** - Bot and spam sessions, 9% of raw chat sessions, are removed

Use the strongest reasoning model your organisation approves to build the backtest harness. Then the forecast and its typical miss run from trusted data with no AI on the page. See [Do you use AI to generate useful dashboards with insights?](/ai-dashboards-with-insights).
