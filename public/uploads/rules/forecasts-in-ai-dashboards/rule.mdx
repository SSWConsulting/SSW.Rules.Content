An AI assistant adds a smooth forecast line in minutes. A warehouse lead rosters staff around it, although nobody knows how far it usually misses. Meanwhile, a half-logged current week looks like a collapse beside a complete week.

**Replay the past before choosing a forecast and show its observed error beside the result.** A method name or a confident AI explanation is not evidence of forecast quality.

<endIntro />

The Northwind Traders dashboards and backtest results below are example data, not measured product performance.

## Replay the past before you pick a method

At each historical cutoff, forecast using only information available then. Match the real forecast horizon: with complete data through 11 October, the week 19–25 October is two steps ahead. [Rolling-origin cross-validation](https://otexts.com/fpp3/toolbox.html#sec-tscv) explains this approach.

Compare the last complete week, a recent average and a seasonal baseline with the proposed model. Score every method on the same dates, horizon and error definition. Choose a metric that reflects the decision; percentage errors are unsuitable for zero forecasts and can behave poorly near zero.

In Northwind's illustrative 52-week selection backtest, the 80th percentile of `abs(actual - forecast) / abs(forecast)` is 20% for the last complete week, 17% for a four-week average, 15% for seasonal plus growth, 14% for a library model and 9% when known promotions and pre-orders are included. These example results do not establish a generally best method.

## Show the error band and check it on later weeks

Northwind's saved forecast is 10,170 orders. A ±9% empirical band is 9,255–11,085 after rounding: 42 of the 52 selection errors fall within 9% of their forecasts. Label it **Past error band**, not a guaranteed prediction interval.

Freeze the method and band, then check them on later weeks excluded from selection. In this example, 11 of 13 holdout outcomes fall inside the fixed band. Show that count and continue checking coverage; thirteen weeks provide limited evidence. Plan staffing around plausible misses, and withhold a forecast when its errors make it unhelpful for the decision.

## Use only inputs known at the cutoff

Promotions, pre-orders and booked leave can help when they were known at forecast time. Store when each record became available, including later revisions. Test the boundary by injecting a late record and confirming the replay excludes it; recompute transformations within each training window so they cannot see the future.

Keep incomplete periods visibly marked **Logged so far**. Check source coverage and known exclusions before interpreting a forecast; [missing data](/show-missing-and-stale-data-on-dashboards) must not become a fall in demand.

## Let trusted data run it

Use AI to help write and test the forecasting code. Save the method, cutoff rules and tests in source control, then run that code on trusted data without asking a language model to invent the figures. Show the issue date, data cutoff and forecast period on the tile. [Traceable dashboard calculations](/ai-dashboards-with-insights) keep it repeatable after the conversation ends.
