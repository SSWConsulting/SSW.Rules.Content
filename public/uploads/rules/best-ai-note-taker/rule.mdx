Everyone has an AI note taker now, and almost nobody checked which one they picked. One tool sends a visible bot into your client call. Another keeps every recording in a US cloud until you delete it. A third trains its models on your meetings by default.

Choosing the first tool that shows up in a search, or the one a colleague installed, puts confidential conversations at risk and can leave attendees feeling secretly recorded. A few minutes of comparison up front avoids a privacy incident later.

<endIntro />

## What to compare before you pick

Every AI note taker can summarise a meeting. The differences that matter are about trust and fit:

* **Bot or no bot** - A bot joins the call as a visible participant, which tells everyone it is being recorded. A bot-free tool captures audio from your own device, so you must tell people yourself
* **Where the data goes** - Check where transcripts are stored, whether the audio is kept, and how long it is retained
* **AI training** - Does the vendor train its models on your meetings by default, and can you opt out?
* **Meeting platform** - Does it work with Teams, Zoom, Google Meet and in-person meetings?
* **Price and limits** - Free plans often cap minutes, history or the number of meetings
* **Output** - Summaries, action items and integrations with the tools you already use

*Prices below were checked on 25 November 2026 and are in US dollars unless stated. Plans change often, so confirm on each vendor's pricing page.*

## Option 1: Microsoft Teams Intelligent Recap (⭐ recommended for Teams meetings)

🔗 [Intelligent recap in Teams](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/intelligent-recap-calls-meetings)

**✅ Pros:**

* No extra bot, app or account - it is built into Teams
* Microsoft states that customer data isn't logged or used for any AI model training or testing
* AI notes are stored in participants' Exchange Online mailboxes, and transcripts and recordings sit in the organiser's Exchange Online and OneDrive or SharePoint. They follow your meeting recording retention policy
* Admins control recording and transcription through Teams meeting policies

**❌ Cons:**

* Needs a Teams Premium licence (AU$15 per user per month, paid yearly, excluding GST) or a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence
* Only works inside Teams (meetings, events and calls), so it does not help with Zoom, Google Meet or in-person meetings
* Your admin must turn on transcription, and recording too for the full experience. Without a recording, recap loses the recording, speakers, topics and chapters

**Best for:** Internal and client meetings that happen in Teams.

## Option 2: Granola

🔗 [granola.ai](https://www.granola.ai/)

**✅ Pros:**

* Bot-free - it never joins the call as a participant. The desktop app captures your device audio and works with Zoom, Google Meet and Teams, and the mobile app is built for in-person meetings
* Doesn't store meeting audio, only the transcript and your notes (on mobile, audio is cached temporarily so it can be transcribed after the meeting)
* SOC 2 Type 2 certified, with the report available from its Trust Center
* Free Basic plan, then Business at $14 per user per month with unlimited history and integrations such as Notion, HubSpot, Attio and Zapier
* Notes are private until you share them

**❌ Cons:**

* You have to start it manually for each meeting, and you must tell attendees yourself because no bot appears in the participant list
* Notes are stored in the United States
* Granola trains on your anonymised data unless you opt out in Settings (Enterprise has training off by default)
* The free Basic plan limits meeting history to 30 days

**Best for:** In-person meetings, ad hoc calls, and people who don't want a bot in the room.

## Option 3: Fathom

🔗 [fathom.ai](https://www.fathom.ai/)

**✅ Pros:**

* The free plan includes unlimited recordings, transcription and AI summaries
* Choice of bot capture or bot-free capture
* SOC 2 Type II audited, HIPAA and GDPR compliant
* Fathom's AI providers are contractually not allowed to train on your data
* Team plan from $15 per user per month (billed annually) adds a shared workspace and team search

**❌ Cons:**

* Bot-free capture is a beta feature that is currently Mac only
* All data is stored in the United States
* Fathom uses de-identified customer data to improve its own models unless you opt out in Settings
* Fathom became part of Superhuman in September 2026, so pricing and features may change

**Best for:** Individuals and small teams who want a capable free tool.

## Option 4: Fireflies

🔗 [fireflies.ai](https://fireflies.ai/)

**✅ Pros:**

* Works with Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams and more, with transcription in 100+ languages (many are still in beta)
* Can record without a bot using its Mac and Windows desktop app, or a Chrome extension (Google Meet only)
* Fireflies states it does not use customer data to train AI models, and its AI vendors are contractually barred from training on it
* SOC 2 Type II and ISO 42001 certified, and GDPR compliant
* Free plan, then Pro from $10 per seat per month (billed annually)
* Strong integrations and analytics for teams with lots of meetings

**❌ Cons:**

* A bot joins by default, so attendees see it
* The free plan has limited storage and AI summaries
* Private Storage for data residency, SSO, SCIM and custom retention are Enterprise only

**Best for:** Teams with a high volume of meetings who need integrations.

## Option 5: Otter

🔗 [otter.ai](https://otter.ai/)

**✅ Pros:**

* Low entry price - Pro is $8.33 per user per month (billed annually)
* Live transcription, plus iOS and Android apps
* SOC 2 Type 2 report
* Desktop app for Mac and Windows that records without a bot
* Free Basic plan with 300 transcription minutes a month

**❌ Cons:**

* Otter's privacy policy says it trains its own AI on de-identified audio recordings and transcriptions, and we could not find a training opt-out in the policy
* The free plan caps conversations at 30 minutes, and Pro caps them at 90 minutes
* SSO, SCIM and HIPAA are Enterprise only
* The assistant (OtterPilot) joins your Zoom, Teams or Google Meet call by default and is visible. Otter also has a Mac and Windows desktop app that records without a bot, so you must tell attendees yourself

**Best for:** Personal use where meetings aren't confidential.

## Option 6: Read AI

🔗 [read.ai](https://www.read.ai/)

**✅ Pros:**

* Works with Zoom, Teams and Google Meet, with apps for Windows, macOS, Android and iOS
* Includes a meeting coach and engagement metrics, and summarises email and messaging too
* Read AI says it doesn't train its models on your meetings unless you opt in to its Customer Experience Program
* Free plan, then Pro at $15 per user per month (billed annually)
* A Windows and Mac desktop app records audio without a bot
* Enterprise+ adds HIPAA, SAML, SCIM, custom data retention and EU or Canadian hosting

**❌ Cons:**

* The free plan is limited to 5 meetings a month and 1 hour per meeting
* An assistant joins scheduled calls by default, so attendees see it. The bot-free desktop app is audio only, with no video or coaching metrics, and you must tell attendees yourself
* Data is stored in the United States by default, and Read AI does not publish an Australian hosting option
* The security controls sit in the top Enterprise+ tier ($29.75 per user per month billed annually), which needs at least 5 licences

**Best for:** Teams who want coaching and insights from their meetings.

## Which one should you use?

* **Meeting in Teams?** Use Teams Intelligent Recap. It needs no new vendor, bot or approval
* **In person, or on another platform, and you don't want a bot?** Use Granola
* **Want to try something for free?** Start with Fathom
* **Lots of meetings and a CRM to feed?** Look at Fireflies
* **Confidential client conversations?** Avoid any tool that trains on your meetings by default, and check your client contract first

## Always ask for consent

Whichever tool you pick, tell everyone before it starts listening. A bot makes this obvious. A bot-free tool makes it your job, because nothing shows in the participant list. Follow [Do you ask for consent to record a Teams meeting?](/consent-to-record) and say it out loud at the start of the meeting.

## Turn the notes into action

A summary is only useful if someone acts on it. After the meeting, check the notes, then turn the action items into tasks. See [Do you use a scribe to capture action items in meetings?](/creating-action-items) for how, including SSW's own [YakShaver](https://yakshaver.ai/).
