An AI feature's monthly bill nearly doubles, and the dashboard shows a red arrow. Is that waste, or is the feature doing twice the useful work? The total can't tell you, so the team either cuts a feature that was paying off or ignores spend that wasn't.

That's why you should **track AI spend in total to stay on budget, and per useful outcome to see whether it's worth it**.

## Track the total for the budget

The total keeps spend predictable. Put a budget alert on it, and alert on throttling and failures too, because cost data arrives late. See [Do you set alerts on AI usage, failures and throttling?](/ai-service-alerts). For the AI tools your developers use, see [Do you monitor AI cost and usage?](/monitor-ai-cost-and-usage).

## Track the cost per useful outcome for value

Divide what a feature spends by the useful results it produces:

* **Pick one useful outcome per feature** - For example, a product description published without edits, or a support chat resolved without a person. Tokens and requests count activity, not results
* **Use the same days for both** - Divide spend by outcomes over the same window, and include the cost of rejected attempts
* **Mark model and prompt changes** - A labelled marker helps readers investigate a step in the line

If spend rises and the cost per outcome falls, the feature is doing more useful work. If both rise, find out why.

In the Northwind Traders example below, spend on AI product descriptions nearly doubled after a gift-basket range was added and the prompt got longer. Accepted descriptions (published without edits) grew faster, so each one now costs $1.21 instead of $1.46.
