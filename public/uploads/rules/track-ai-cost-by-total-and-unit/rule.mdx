An AI feature's spend nearly doubles and its dashboard turns red. Is that waste, or does the feature produce more than twice as much useful work? A total or cost per request cannot answer that decision.

**Track total AI spend for the budget and cost per useful outcome for value.** Define useful before comparing models or prompts.

## Track the total for the budget

Keep an overall spend limit and an owner for investigating alerts. Also [alert on failures and throttling](/ai-service-alerts): [cost data can arrive late](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/understand-cost-mgt-data). A budget alert is not a real-time service-health check.

For developer subscriptions and token activity, see [monitoring AI cost and usage](/monitor-ai-cost-and-usage). This rule adds a measure of useful output from an AI feature.

## Define and count useful work

Choose a measurable acceptance criterion: a description published without edits, or a support case resolved without reopening during a defined follow-up period. A token, request or generated draft counts activity, not success.

Divide all attributable feature costs, including rejected attempts, retries and failed calls, by accepted outcomes counted once. Keep the same currency, cost basis and attribution policy across periods. State whether the figure covers model charges only or also hosting and human review; omitted costs limit the value claim.

Align costs with their outcomes. If publishing or resolution takes days, use a completed cohort and its associated costs, or flag a provisional period until outcomes mature. **No accepted outcomes** is a visible state, not a zero unit cost or division by zero.

## Save the calculation, then compare periods

Keep `cost_per_accepted_description_v1`, its joins and its acceptance definition in source control. A reviewed one-off agent query can explore the data; a recurring daily tile uses the saved calculation. [Reconcile both spend and accepted counts independently](/check-dashboard-totals-against-the-source) before drawing advice.

Northwind compares completed product-description cohorts for 14–20 September and 9–15 October. The example uses billed model charges only, including rejected drafts:

* Spend rises from **$443 to $868**, up 96%
* Accepted descriptions rise from **304 to 720**, up 137%
* Cost per accepted description falls from **$1.46 to $1.21**, down 17%

Lower unit cost does not excuse a budget breach or lower quality. Keep acceptance criteria comparable, mark changes and investigate shifts in work mix before attributing the improvement to a model or prompt. Run [quality evals](/run-evals-on-ai-features) before accepting a cheaper alternative.

Put both measures on [a traceable dashboard](/ai-dashboards-with-insights) so readers can distinguish affordability from value.
