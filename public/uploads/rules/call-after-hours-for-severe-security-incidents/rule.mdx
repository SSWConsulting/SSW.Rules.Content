At 9pm you spot an admin account signing in from another country. You hover over the security lead's number, decide it's probably not worth waking them, and send a Teams message instead. Their phone is on Do Not Disturb. They read it at 8am, and the attacker has had 11 hours.

For a severe security incident, just call, whatever the time. It's okay if they don't pick up. What isn't okay is getting stuck deciding whether to try.

<endIntro />

## Why should you call rather than wait?

A call costs them a few seconds of sleep. Not calling can cost you the whole night.

* **Notifications are muted** - Quiet Hours in Teams and Do Not Disturb on phones silence chat and email notifications until morning, so a message can sit unseen for hours
* **Every hour counts** - An attacker with a live session can create accounts, pull data and cover their tracks while you wait
* **Trying is the job** - You don't need permission to call. You need a severe issue and a phone

## What if they don't pick up?

That's fine. You've still done the right thing.

* **Leave a voicemail** - Say what's happening and what you've already done, so they can act the moment they see it
* **Try the backup** - If there's a second contact, call them next
* **Keep going** - Contain what you can, like disabling the account, and record it as you go. A missed call is not a reason to stop

## What counts as severe enough to call?

If waiting until 8am makes it worse, call. For example:

* **Active compromise** - An account takeover or an attacker inside a system right now
* **Data exposure** - Customer or personal data being taken or publicly reachable
* **Live leaked secret** - A production key or password that's leaked and still works
* **Destructive activity** - Ransomware, mass deletion, or production down because of an attack

A phishing email nobody clicked, a vulnerability scan finding or a dependency alert can wait. Raise them during business hours.

## How do you agree on it before an incident?

Ask your security lead now, so nobody has to wonder at 9pm. When we asked ours, the answer was simple: "If it's important, just call."

Then write it down where everyone can find it:

* **Who to call** - The on-call person if you have a roster, otherwise a named primary and a backup
* **Which number** - Their mobile, not their desk phone or Teams
* **Let the call through** - Ask them to add the on-call or team number to their phone's Do Not Disturb allowed list, so it rings even when the phone is silenced
