At 9pm you spot an admin account signing in from another country. You don't want to wake the security lead, so you send a Teams message and an email. Their phone is on Do Not Disturb. They read it at 8am, and the attacker has had 11 hours.

For a severe security incident, pick up the phone, whatever the time. Agree on what counts as severe and who to call before anything goes wrong, so nobody has to decide at 9pm whether it's "worth" waking someone.

<endIntro />

## Why should you call instead of sending a message?

After hours, messages wait. A call, and especially a second call, is far more likely to get through.

* **Notifications are muted** - Quiet Hours in Teams and Do Not Disturb on phones silence chat and email notifications until morning
* **Every hour counts** - An attacker with a live session can create accounts, pull data and cover their tracks while your message sits unread
* **You know it landed** - A call tells you someone has picked it up. A message tells you nothing until they reply

## What counts as severe enough to call?

If waiting until 8am makes it worse, call. For example:

* **Active compromise** - An account takeover or an attacker inside a system right now
* **Data exposure** - Customer or personal data being taken or publicly reachable
* **Live leaked secret** - A production key or password that's leaked and still works
* **Destructive activity** - Ransomware, mass deletion, or production down because of an attack

A phishing email nobody clicked, a vulnerability scan finding or a dependency alert can wait. Raise them during business hours.

## How do you agree on it before an incident?

Ask your security lead now, not at 9pm. When we asked ours, the answer was simple: "If it's important, just call."

Then write it down where everyone can find it:

* **Who to call** - The on-call person if you have a roster, otherwise a named primary and a backup
* **Which number** - Their mobile, not their desk phone or Teams
* **What if they don't answer** - Call twice within 3 minutes, leave a voicemail, then call the backup after 10 minutes
* **Let the call through** - Ask them to add the on-call or team number to their phone's Do Not Disturb allowed list, so it rings even when the phone is silenced
