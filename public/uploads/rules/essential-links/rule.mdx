Most people visit a website for one thing, like a phone number, a price, or a sign-in page. If they have to hunt through menus or scroll to the footer to find it, many give up and go somewhere else.

Put the links people need most in your navigation bar, so they're one click away from every page.

<endIntro />

## What to put in your navigation bar

* **A logo that links home** - People expect the logo in the top-left corner to take them back to the home page
* **Contact us** - Link to a page with a phone number, not just a form (see [making your contact details easy to find](/ensure-that-your-contact-details-are-easy-to-find))
* **Your main call to action** - The one thing you want visitors to do, like "Book a meeting" or "Start a free trial", styled as a button
* **Search** - People who know what they want can skip the menus
* **Sign in** - If your site has an app or customer portal, returning users can get straight in

Keep the bar short. Other links, like careers, news, and directions, belong in a dropdown or the footer. If you have many links, group them in a [mega menu](/do-you-use-mega-menu-navigation-to-improve-usability).

## Keep them visible on mobile

On small screens, the navigation usually collapses into a [hamburger menu](/hamburger-menu). Keep your most important link, like Contact us or your main call to action, visible next to it, so it's still one tap away.
