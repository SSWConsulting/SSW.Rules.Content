An AI assistant builds an operations dashboard in an afternoon, and it looks finished. Then the support lead asks where the 77% accuracy figure came from, and nobody can answer: which system, how many checks, or whether the AI worked it out when the page loaded. The AI commentary underneath has already recommended a rollback.

That's why you should **use a capable AI model to build the dashboard, let trusted sources run it, and bring AI back on a schedule for insights**.

## Build for one audience, in your own branding

Start with one audience and the few questions they need answered, not "show all our data".

Use **the strongest reasoning model your organisation approves** to build it and to run the investigations. Both need judgement: what to measure, what to leave off, and when evidence is too thin to call a change.

Give the model your design system (colours, type, header, product names) so the dashboard carries your company's branding, not a generic AI look. See [Do you use AI to speed up frontend design?](/ai-with-design-system). Branding earns attention; only traceable numbers earn trust.

## Connect trusted sources, then run without AI

Every tile and chart reads straight from a trusted source: the database, a system API, a billing export or an approved report. No hard-coded figures, leftover placeholders, numbers typed into a prompt, or model estimates posing as actuals.

Each number shows its source and data age, and every percentage has its count (**47/61 · 77%**). To check the wiring, pick a range you know has different records, refresh, and compare the result with the source.

Opening or refreshing the dashboard pulls new data and makes no AI call. The page stays fast, cheap and consistent between AI runs, even when the AI service is down. See [Do you monitor AI cost and usage?](/monitor-ai-cost-and-usage).

If a feed stops, say so. Northwind's customer ratings feed paused on 2 Sep, so the page shows a note, not a confident 0.0 stars.

## Bring AI back on a schedule for insights

Schedule an AI run daily or weekly, matching how often someone acts on the numbers. It does what a query can't: it investigates a change before calling it out, then writes a few ranked insights, not one per tile, saying what changed, why it matters and what to check next. Northwind's top insight says description acceptance rose from 30/50 to 44/52 after the 7 Oct prompt change, and suggests testing the prompt on the gift-basket range.

* **Date the insights separately** - The panel shows when AI last ran, apart from the live tiles
* **Quote recorded figures** - The commentary uses figures from the sources, and AI must never invent actuals
* **Label what AI derives** - Counts the scheduled run derives, such as sorting 301 chats into themes, are stored with their date, labelled as AI-derived and spot-checked by a person before they're shown. See [Do you run evals on your AI features?](/run-evals-on-ai-features)
* **Allow "no clear change yet"** - A weekly sample of about 60 checks can't confirm a small move, so the AI must not invent a story or blame the nearest release. See [Do your dashboards show whether a change is real?](/dashboard-changes-real-or-noise)

## Use an agent for a one-off question

A one-off question doesn't need a dashboard; let an agent answer it. See [Do you give your AI agents access to your database?](/ai-database-access). Once someone checks a number daily, give it a proper source-backed tile.
