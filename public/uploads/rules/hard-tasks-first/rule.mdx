It's tempting to start the day with easy tasks, like clearing emails or making small fixes. They feel productive, but the hard task is usually the most valuable one, and it keeps getting pushed to tomorrow.

Do the hard thing first, while you're fresh and focused. The rest of the day gets easier, and the work that matters gets done.

## Why hard tasks matter

Hard tasks need the most thinking and often unblock other people, so they usually have the most value. Leave them until late in the day and they get done tired, rushed, or not at all.

"Hard first" still means the most important hard task, not any hard task. See [Priorities - Do you complete work in order of importance?](/work-in-order-of-importance-aka-priorities)

## How to tackle hard tasks first

### 1. Pick it the day before

Before you finish for the day, choose tomorrow's hardest task. You'll start the morning knowing what to do, instead of looking for something easy. See [Do you plan the night before what you are doing the next day?](/plan-tomorrow-today)

### 2. Break it down

Split the task into smaller steps. A big task feels daunting, but a first step you can start in a few minutes doesn't.

### 3. Eat the frog

[Eat the frog](https://todoist.com/productivity-methods/eat-the-frog) works well if you struggle to get started on hard tasks:

1. Identify the frog - Find the hardest task in your backlog (aka the frog)
2. Eat it - Do it as the first task in the morning
3. Repeat - Eat the frog every day

### 4. Ask for help early

If you're stuck, don't wait. A quick call with someone who has done it before can save you hours.
