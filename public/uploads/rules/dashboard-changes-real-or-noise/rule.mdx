A support dashboard turns red because agreement falls from 86% to 77%, and its AI insight blames the nearby release. A leaderboard crowns questions with six checks each. Small samples can move sharply by chance, but the page presents a verdict.

**Show the evidence behind each rate and distinguish a target breach from evidence of a change.** Use a planned comparison before calling a rise or fall clear.

## Put counts and uncertainty beside rates

Write **47/61 · 77% agreement**, and define agreement: the AI and a support agent give matching answers under a written rule for deciding whether their answers match. For independent sampled checks, a [90% Wilson interval](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm) is about 67–85%. An interval describes sampling uncertainty; it cannot repair biased selection.

## Plan comparisons before colouring changes

Northwind plans two weekly comparisons. A [two-sided pooled two-proportion z-test](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section3/prc33.htm) gives:

* **Support: no clear change yet** — 47/61 versus 55/64, p ≈ 0.20
* **Descriptions: clear rise** — 44/52 versus 30/50, p ≈ 0.005

Running several comparisons creates more chances for false alarms. [Holm adjustment](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/p.adjust.html) controls that risk across the planned comparisons: here the two p-values become about 0.20 and 0.011. Northwind requires adjusted p below 0.05 and a change of at least ten percentage points before colouring a change. These are its planned decision settings, not universal thresholds. The description rise meets both. The 90% display interval describes uncertainty in one rate; the 0.05 test threshold is a separate decision setting. Do not infer the test result from whether two displayed intervals overlap.

Keep the operational target badge separate: support is **Below 80% target** and still **No clear change yet**. Investigate the disagreements and coverage, but keep the change status neutral while collecting more independent checks. Plan sample size for the smallest change that would alter a decision; use longer non-overlapping periods when weekly counts are too small. An unclear result does not prove there was no change.

Mark relevant prompt, model and data changes. A marker shows timing, not causation; use [evals before shipping a proposed fix](/run-evals-on-ai-features).

## Check the sample before trusting the test

The test assumes independent checks from comparable populations. Repeated chats, shared customers or review batches can break that assumption. Use a method suited to those clusters; for sparse independent counts, consider an exact test instead of the large-sample approximation. Repeated daily looks need a monitoring method that accounts for repeated testing, not a fresh weekly p-value each day.

Keep [random and targeted checks separate, with coverage](/show-coverage-on-ai-quality-dashboards). A complaints-only sample measures complaints, however precise its interval looks.

Use established library functions, such as Statsmodels [Wilson intervals](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.proportion.proportion_confint.html) and [two-sided proportion tests](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.proportion.proportions_ztest.html), with the settings saved beside the metric. For the displayed interval, select Wilson with `alpha=0.10`; the change test uses a two-sided alternative.

## Make rankings earn their place

Northwind requires ten checks before ranking and orders best results by the lower Wilson bound. Thus 58/60 (90–99%) ranks above 19/19 (88–100%); 6/6 (69–100%) stays **Not ranked yet**. Ten is a display rule, not proof of adequate statistical power.
