A support dashboard turns red because 77% agreement is below its 80% target, and its AI insight blames the nearby release. A leaderboard crowns questions with six checks each. Small samples can move sharply by chance, but the page presents a verdict.

**Show the evidence behind each rate and distinguish a target breach from evidence of a change.** Use a planned comparison before calling a rise or fall clear.

## Put counts and uncertainty beside rates

Write **47/61 · 77% agreement** and publish the comparison checklist. In this example, strict agreement requires the AI and reviewer answers to contain the same required facts and recommended action, including date and number formats. A formatting-only difference still counts as a disagreement.

For independent sampled checks, a [90% Wilson interval](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm) is about 67–85%. An interval describes sampling uncertainty; it cannot repair biased selection.

## Plan comparisons before colouring changes

Northwind plans two weekly comparisons. A [two-sided pooled two-proportion z-test](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section3/prc33.htm) checks how surprising the observed gap would be if the two populations had the same underlying rate. It gives:

* **Support: no clear change yet** — 47/61 versus 55/64, p ≈ 0.20
* **Descriptions: clear rise** — 44/52 versus 30/50, p ≈ 0.005

Running several comparisons creates more chances for false alarms. [Holm adjustment](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/p.adjust.html) controls that risk across the planned comparisons: here the two p-values become about 0.20 and 0.011.

Northwind requires adjusted p below 0.05 and a change of at least ten percentage points before colouring a change. These are its planned decision settings, not universal thresholds. The description rise meets both. Choose your own minimum practical change as the smallest shift that would trigger different work.

The 90% display interval describes uncertainty in one rate; the 0.05 test threshold is a separate decision setting. Do not infer the test result from whether two displayed intervals overlap.

## Keep target status separate from change status

Keep the operational target badge separate: support is **Below 80% target** and still **No clear change yet**. Investigate the disagreements and coverage, but keep the change status neutral while collecting more independent checks. Plan sample size for the smallest change that would alter a decision; use longer non-overlapping periods when weekly counts are too small. An unclear result does not prove there was no change.

Mark relevant prompt, model and data changes. A marker shows timing, not causation; use [evals before shipping a proposed fix](/run-evals-on-ai-features).

## Check the sample before trusting the test

The test assumes independent checks from comparable populations. Repeated chats, shared customers or review batches can break that assumption. Use a method suited to those clusters. When there are only a few successes or failures, use an exact test rather than a z-test, which relies on having enough of both. Repeated daily looks need a monitoring method that accounts for repeated testing, not a fresh weekly p-value each day.

Keep [random and targeted checks separate, with coverage](/show-coverage-on-ai-quality-dashboards). A complaints-only sample measures complaints, however precise its interval looks.

Use established library functions, such as Statsmodels [Wilson intervals](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.proportion.proportion_confint.html) and [two-sided proportion tests](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.proportion.proportions_ztest.html), with the settings saved beside the metric. For the displayed interval, select Wilson with `alpha=0.10`; the change test uses a two-sided alternative.

## Make rankings earn their place

The question leaderboard uses a separate fixed audit of 103 checks from 1 September–14 October 2026, rather than the weekly support sample of 61 checks. Each audit check belongs to one of six questions: 60 order-status checks, 19 opening-hours checks and six checks for each of the other four questions.

Northwind requires ten checks before ranking and orders best results by the lower Wilson bound. Thus 58/60 (90–99%) ranks above 19/19 (88–100%); each 6/6 (69–100%) stays **Not ranked yet**. Ten is a display rule, not proof of adequate statistical power.
