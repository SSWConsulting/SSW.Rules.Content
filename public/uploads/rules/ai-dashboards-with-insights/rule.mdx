An AI assistant builds an operations dashboard in an afternoon. It looks finished, but nobody can trace its 77% accuracy figure to a source or find out how many answers were checked. The AI commentary already recommends a rollback.

That's why you should **build AI dashboards on traceable data and require evidence for their insights**.

## Build for one audience, in your own branding

Start with one audience and the few questions they need answered, not "show all our data". Give the model your design system (colours, type, header, product names) so the dashboard looks like your company, not a generic AI page. See [Do you use AI to speed up frontend design?](/ai-with-design-system).

## Refresh the figures without an AI call

Every tile reads straight from a trusted source: the database, a system API, a billing export or an approved report. No hard-coded figures, numbers typed into a prompt, or model estimates posing as actuals. Each number shows its source and data age.

Tools such as [Claude Dashboards](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/17454700-get-started-with-claude-dashboards) connect to your data warehouse and show the query and last refresh behind each chart, so you can check what every tile counts.

Opening or refreshing the dashboard makes no AI call, so the figures remain available even when the AI service is down. If a feed stops, say so. Northwind's customer ratings feed paused on 2 Sep, so the page shows a note, not a confident 0.0 stars.

Before anyone relies on the numbers, work through:

* [Do you remove fake data and dead controls from AI-built dashboards?](/remove-fake-data-from-ai-built-dashboards)
* [Do you check your dashboard totals against the source?](/check-dashboard-totals-against-the-source)
* [Do your dashboards say when data is missing or stale?](/show-missing-and-stale-data-on-dashboards)

## Show whether a change is real

Small samples move a lot by chance, so show the count behind each percentage and colour a tile only when the change is clear. See [Do your dashboards show whether a change is real?](/dashboard-changes-real-or-noise).

## Bring AI back on a schedule for insights

Schedule an AI run daily or weekly, matching how often someone acts on the numbers. Give it the recorded figures and relevant change history. Ask it to highlight changes worth investigating, with a source and a suggested next check for each. Northwind's top insight notes that description acceptance rose after the 7 Oct prompt change.

* **Date the insights** - The panel shows when AI last ran, apart from the live tiles
* **Quote recorded figures** - AI uses figures from the sources and never invents actuals
* **Label what AI derives** - Counts AI works out, such as sorting 301 chats into themes, are labelled and spot-checked by a person. A person agreeing with 19 of 20 AI classifications shows agreement, not proven correctness. See [Do you run evals on your AI features?](/run-evals-on-ai-features)
* **Allow "no clear change yet"** - When the evidence is thin, AI must not invent a story or blame the nearest release

## Add forecasts and cost when you need them

* **Forecasts** - Backtest a forecast before it goes on the page. See [Do you use forecasts in AI dashboards?](/forecasts-in-ai-dashboards)
* **AI cost** - Track total spend and the cost of each useful outcome. See [Do you track AI cost by total and by unit?](/track-ai-cost-by-total-and-unit)
