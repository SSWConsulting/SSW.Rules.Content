If you've ever recorded a great video on your phone only to discover that the audio is muffled, echoey, or overwhelmed by background noise, you know how frustrating poor sound quality can be. 

Viewers will often tolerate average video quality, but they are much less forgiving of poor audio. That's why using an external microphone is a simple best practice that dramatically improves the quality of mobile phone recordings.

<endIntro />

Good audio makes your message easier to understand, keeps viewers engaged, and gives your videos a more polished, professional feel.

\## Why use an external microphone?

While modern smartphones have excellent cameras, their built-in microphones are designed for general-purpose use rather than high-quality recording. An external microphone offers several advantages:

\- Produces clearer, more natural speech

\- Reduces background noise and room echo

\- Captures consistent audio even when the phone is further away

\- Makes videos sound more professional and trustworthy

Whether you're recording training videos, interviews, presentations, or social media content, better audio has a bigger impact than upgrading your camera.

\## Choose the right microphone

The best microphone depends on the type of recording you're doing.

\### Lavalier (lapel) microphone

A lavalier microphone clips onto your clothing, keeping the microphone close to your mouth. This is ideal for talking directly to the camera while moving around.

\### Wireless microphone

Wireless microphones provide greater freedom of movement and are perfect for demonstrations, presentations, and outdoor recordings.

\### Shotgun microphone

A shotgun microphone is useful when you want to record someone in front of the camera without attaching a microphone to them. It works best when positioned close to the speaker.

\## Additional tips for better audio

Using a microphone is only part of the solution. To achieve the best possible sound:

\- Record in a quiet environment whenever possible

\- Turn off noisy air conditioners or fans if practical

\- Monitor audio with headphones before recording important content

\- Keep the microphone close to the speaker's mouth

\- Use a windscreen ("deadcat") when recording outdoors

\## Remember

People will forgive slightly imperfect video, but they quickly stop watching when audio is difficult to hear. Investing in a good external microphone is one of the easiest and most cost-effective ways to improve the quality of mobile phone recordings.
