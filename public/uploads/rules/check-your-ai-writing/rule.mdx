A fluent draft can repeat itself, overstate a result, or promise something you did not intend. Review AI-assisted writing before sending it, just as you would review any draft.

Check its accuracy, meaning, and usefulness to the reader. Familiar phrasing or punctuation does not establish who wrote it.

## What should you check before sending?

Read the whole draft, then investigate specific problems:

* **Unsupported claims** - Open the source and check that it supports the statement, including its date, scope, and uncertainty
* **Praise without information** - Replace vague claims of importance with a supported explanation, or remove them
* **Unnecessary repetition** - Cut material that repeats a point without helping the reader
* **Tone and presentation** - Choose language and formatting that fit the audience and channel; formal writing can be appropriate
* **Empty contrasts** - Keep a contrast when it explains a real distinction, rather than adding emphasis to a claim nobody disputed
* **Unclear reasoning** - Ask what follows from the evidence, and whether a metaphor or transition helps readers follow the argument

Three-part lists, em dashes, long sentences, and polished structure can all work well. Change them when they cause a problem in context. A house style can prefer different punctuation without treating it as evidence of authorship. See [the types of dashes](/dashes/) and [how to assess suspected AI authorship fairly](/assess-ai-authorship-fairly/).

## How can you guide the draft?

Give the AI your notes, audience, purpose, and a relevant writing sample. Specify whether you want an outline, suggested edits, or a full draft. Save reusable style preferences where your tool supports them.

## Should you use a humanizer?

An editing skill such as [humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) can suggest changes. It is optional, and its instructions are not an authorship test or a guarantee that the rewrite preserves meaning.

Compare each suggestion with the original. Check whether a qualification disappeared, a possibility became a promise, or an opinion changed. Read the final version yourself. For a fuller workflow, see [editing AI-assisted writing without changing its meaning](/humanise-ai-generated-content/).
