Often times it can be tempting to say ‘yes’ just simply to be polite or to keep the conversation moving, but this can cause a serious break in communication.

<endIntro />

Watch Adam give this tip to Hark during his first week at SSW!

<youtubeEmbed url="https://www.youtube.com/shorts/4KDQghJL5g8" description="Video: Day 5 at SSW: Say yes when you understand, not to be polite | Tip by Adam Cogan | Tales of Hark (2 min)" />

Imagine a client asks you to change how users log in to their application, but the requirements are unclear.

<boxEmbed
  body={<>
    Instead of asking questions, you reply:
    
    “Yep, sounds good to me!”
  </>}
  figurePrefix="bad"
  figure="Miscommunication between the client and developer"
/>

Now you have an unhappy client, wasted development time, and additional costs caused by a misunderstanding.

Let's try a better approach…

<boxEmbed
  body={<>
    You: “Could you clarify why we're changing the login process?”
    
    Client: “We want users to log in with their email address instead of their username because customers keep forgetting their usernames.”
  </>}
  figurePrefix="good"
  figure="Asking clarifying questions bridges the gap between you and the client"
/>

As a consultant or developer, always ask follow-up questions when something is unclear. For example, if a client requests a search feature, ask whether they want to search by name, category, or both. Similarly, if your manager asks you to prepare a report, clarify what information they want included.

## Asking questions isn't rude

Taking a few extra minutes to clarify expectations can save hours of rework and help you deliver the right solution the first time.
