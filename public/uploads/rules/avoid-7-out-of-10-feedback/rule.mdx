Giving something a "7/10" often says very little. It avoids answering the real question of whether it's actually good enough, replacing a clear opinion with a vague score.

<endIntro />

## Why 7/10 is a cop-out

When someone asks *"how was it?"* or *"is this good?"*, a 7/10 score sounds polite, but is really just sitting on the fence:

* Doesn't say what's wrong
* Doesn't say what's right
* Doesn't say what to do next

It protects the reviewer from an uncomfortable conversation, but it leaves the person asking with nothing they can act on.

<boxEmbed
  body={<>
    **Note:** This isn't a rule against using numbers. Scores like 8/10 or 9/10 can be a great way to recognize good work, especially when paired with specific feedback.
    
    The problem is giving a noncommittal middle score, such as 7/10, without explaining what it means. A clear score backed by actionable feedback is just as useful as a simple pass or fail.
  </>}

## What to do instead

If it's not ready to ship, say so and explain why. If it is ready, say that clearly and specifically.

* **Use " ✅ Pass / ❌ Fail "** when the decision is whether something is ready to go live
* **When you give a score, make it decisive** - An 8 or 9 says something. So does a 2 or 3. A 7 usually doesn't
* **Always justify the score** - Explain exactly what's standing between the score given and a 10. E.g.: *"Not ready to ship because {{ REASON }}"*
* **Be specific** - List the exact things that need to change
