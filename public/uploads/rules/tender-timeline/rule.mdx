A tender is released, the Bid Manager is busy with other bids, and the detailed work starts with less than a week to go. Developers and the tender consultant arrive late, the pricing model is decided in the final days, and the team lodges at 3am. Each tender then runs on gut feel because nobody wrote down when each stage should happen.

<endIntro />

On the day the tender is released, the Bid Manager drafts a timeline that works back from the closing date. It is the core of the bid plan (see [the Tender Team roles](/set-tender-team-roles)) and it shows:

* **When** - the day each stage starts, counted from release and from close
* **Owner** - the one person accountable for the stage
* **Who is pulled in** - the people outside the Bid Team, and for how long, so they can block out the time
* **AI skill** - the stages where an AI skill does the first draft, so everyone knows which output to check rather than write

## Example timeline

This example is for a tender with 15 business days between release and close. Scale the middle stages for shorter or longer tenders, but keep the first 3 days and the last 3 days as they are.

| When | Stage | Owner | Who is pulled in | AI skill |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Read the tender and write the [one-page summary](/tender-one-page-summary) | Bid Manager | - | 🤖 Drafts the summary |
| Day 1 | Fill in the Go/No-Go assessment and draft any clarification questions | Bid Manager | - | 🤖 Drafts the Go/No-Go and questions |
| Day 2 | [Gate 1 - Go/No-Go](/tender-gates) meeting. Decide the pricing model, hosting, and the named developers | Bid Owner | Bid Owner, Sales (1 hour) | - |
| Day 3 | Set the target - Win Strategy and Win Themes | Sales | Tender consultant (half a day) | - |
| Day 3-4 | Record the [Tender Plan video](/tender-plan-video) | Pre-sales Solution Architect | Bid Owner watches (10 min) | - |
| Day 4-11 | Write the response and build the proof of concept (POC) | Bid Writer, Pre-sales Solution Architect | Developers (as approved at Gate 1) | 🤖 Drafts the first version of each section |
| Close - 3 days | Review the draft against the target | Bid Manager | Tender consultant (half a day) | - |
| Close - 2 days | Record the [Tender Summary video](/tender-summary-video) and hold [Gate 2 - Submit](/tender-gates) | Bid Manager | Bid Owner (30 min) | - |
| Close - 1 day | [Final QA check](/tender-final-qa-check) and lodge | Bid Manager | - | 🤖 Runs the QA checklist |
| After close | [Retro](/what-happens-at-retro-meetings) | Bid Manager | Bid Team (30 min) | - |

**Figure: Each row has a day, one owner, and the time you need from people outside the Bid Team**

## Decide the commercials at Gate 1, not in the last days

Pricing model, price, and hosting change the whole response. Decide them at the Gate 1 meeting with the developers who will deliver the work.

If the tender requires a fixed price, the Bid Owner must approve the price in writing before Gate 1 closes. A verbal "yes" given at the last minute is not enough.

## Send clarification questions only after sign-off

Questions to the buyer are often shared with every bidder, so they can give away your strategy. Draft them on day 1 and get a strategic person (Sales or the Bid Owner) to approve them at Gate 1. Send only what you need.

## Track progress against the timeline

At each cadence meeting, the Bid Manager compares progress to the timeline. If a stage slips, move the later dates and tell the people who are pulled in. Do not quietly squeeze the final 3 days.
